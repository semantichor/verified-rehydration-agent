## Q1. What does the buyer-contract promise as delivered?

The buyer-contract promises an execution-neutral, self-contained normative specification of the agent's decision-logic surface for controlled rehydration of persistent context. Per README §"What the specification guarantees" it states 7 structural properties: no restoration before the full verification sequence completes; fail-safe handling with suppression of persistent-context injection on any listed failure; restored persistent state only from the verified and eligible head; tool-state inactive unless a separate activation gate and required approval both succeed; startup read-only until the restoration decision is reached; idempotent recovery; and a deterministic replay baseline (`Verified_Rehydration_Agent_README.md:71`). Per Whitepaper §05 it states 4 structural properties: state verified before use, failures suppressed, freshness confirmed, and safe mode on failure. Concretely the delivered agent-logic surface is the verdict vocabulary (§6), the ordered verification sequence (§7), the failure semantics (§8), the validator catalogue with severities (§10), the decision logic (§11), the workflow state machine and transitions (§12), the gate logic (§13), the evidence contract (§14), and the machine-readable forms (§15) — sufficient to build the restoration gate with no further agent-logic design decisions.

## Q2. What does the buyer-contract explicitly delegate to deployment?

Per README §"What your team must produce" and Specification §16 Deployment obligations, the buyer's engineering team builds 5 companion artifacts that are NOT in the pack and that I do NOT implement: (1) a stable snapshot facility — a stable snapshot lock or deterministic equivalent with observable snapshot-binding evidence; (2) an anti-rollback mechanism — a non-regressing counter or equivalent anchor; (3) a local, versioned policy bundle binding `policy_id`, `V_POLICY`, and `V_COMPAT` compatibility checks; (4) a single-writer persistence control plane — serialized persistent-state mutation paths emitting writer-lock and contention evidence; (5) a readmission authority workflow — an `approver_set`, recovery workflow, readmission-token evidence, and release procedure (`Verified_Rehydration_Agent_README.md:85`). Operator-declared parameters (cadences, procedures, routes, roles) are likewise delegated per the README's "how to read the package when a question arises" layering.

## Q4. Where is the boundary between spec-derivable and deployment-substrate PTs?

The runnable records PT-001..PT-023, PT-025, PT-026, and PT-027 exercise the spec-derivable projection surface — the `next_state(current_state, trigger, validator_passed)` transitions grounded in §12, the verdict-versus-state taxonomy grounded in §6 and D4, and the evidence-field-presence and producer-tag assertions grounded in §14 — and are locally runnable. PT-024 is spec-derivable as a membership assertion (result within {S0 STARTUP_READONLY, S1 SNAPSHOT_LOCKED}) whose exact release target is operator-declared, so it runs under a buyer-declared envelope. PT-028 (`V_COMPAT`) and PT-029 (`V_WRITER_LOCK`) depend on deployment-substrate enforcement surfaces — the local policy-compatibility enforcement point and the single-writer persistence control plane — that the pack delegates to the buyer per README "What your team must produce" and §16 Deployment obligations, and that the Minimal Run Note names as "Bounded external obligations not locally runnable" (`Verified_Rehydration_Agent_Conformance_Tests.md:518`). These two are the deployment-substrate PTs, recorded as SKIP with attestation.
